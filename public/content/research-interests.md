# Language Production
My primary research focus recently has been on language production: how do language users manage to put together sentences in real time? I'm mostly interested in unit size in sentence production. If non-lexicalist theories of language competence tell us we build structures from ever tinier pieces, how do we ever manage to coordinate enough of them to get big multi-clausal level things as fast as we do? I think frameworks that take a flexible, but grammatically driven approach to incrementality will have a lot to tell us about that (see: Tree Adjoining Grammar!)

# Sentence memory and Verbal Working Memory
In addition to production, my current research program is focused on examining memory for previously encoded sentences. How do comprehenders index sentences in memory? What is the natural data structure that the grammar affords sentence memory? How does long-term memory for encountered utterances help production processes?

# The Brain
The brain probably does all the thinking we need, so to understand linguistic thought better I study the brain. I'm interested in how organizing principles of a deeply interconnected and complex system like the human brain uniquely gives rise to natural language structures. I'm currently interested in the computational properties of neural oscillations and how waves can integrate with spikes in neurocognition.

# Syntax
I'm interested in explicit computational models of syntax, Tree Adjoining Grammars being my current obsession (though Minimalist Grammars got me into computation). I'm also deeply invested in non-lexicalist theories of syntax that break down the traditional lexicon-syntax distinction in different ways.
